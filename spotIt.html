Spot It

Prototype · ages 8–9 · sample content

What this prototype tests

One scenario, one age band (8–9, high support): a confident AI helper makes one checkable mistake. The child moves through Notice, Investigate, Judge and Reflect, then applies the same habit to a new tool.

Design choices

  • The two meters separate sounding sure from being checked, so the child sees that confidence is not evidence.
  • Hints follow a four-step ladder (ask, point, compare, model). They are worded as questions, never give the answer, and carry no penalty.
  • Sami explains how it works in plain words: it puts words together the way they usually go, and it can mix things up.
  • The last question uses a brand-new tool to check transfer of the habit, not recall of the camel fact.

What I would measure first

  • Share of children who open two or more sources before judging.
  • Transfer item correct, compared before and after the scenario.
  • Hint use by level, to see where the scaffolding is too heavy or too light.

Grounding

  • OECD and European Commission AILit Framework (final, June 2026): Engage with AI.
  • Bastani et al., PNAS: unguarded AI help can hurt later unaided performance. That study used high school math, so treat it as a design caution for this age group.
  • UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children 3.0.
